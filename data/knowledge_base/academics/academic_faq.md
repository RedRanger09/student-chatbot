# Academic FAQ

## 1. What is the minimum attendance requirement for passing a course?
Attendance is critical for academic success. Students must attend at least 75% of all classes to be eligible for exams. Regular participation ensures understanding of core concepts and allows instructors to assess student engagement. If attendance falls below this threshold, students may receive a warning or face failure in the course. Exceptions are made for documented emergencies, but these require prior approval from the academic office.

## 2. How are credit points calculated for theory versus practical courses?
Theory courses typically carry more credit points than practical ones due to their depth of content. For example, a 3-credit theory course might cover 45 hours of material, while a 2-credit lab course focuses on hands-on application. Credit allocation reflects the time and effort required to master each type of learning. Students should consult their schedule to understand how different courses contribute to their total credit load.

## 3. Can I take more than the maximum allowed credits in a semester?
Credit overload is permitted under specific conditions. Students must submit a formal request to the academic office, demonstrating that they can manage the increased workload without compromising academic performance. Approval requires a minimum GPA of 3.5 and no existing backlogs. Overloaded students must also agree to additional tutoring sessions.

## 4. What happens if I fail one subject?
Failing a single course doesn't automatically disqualify you from continuing your program. You'll receive a warning and must retake the course in the next semester. If you fail two subjects, you may be placed on academic probation. Repeated failures could result in suspension, so it's crucial to seek academic advising early.

## 5. How is my CGPA calculated?
CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is determined by averaging all your grade points across completed courses. Each course's grade is multiplied by its credit value, summed up, and then divided by the total credits earned. This provides a holistic view of your academic performance. For example, a 3-credit course with a B+ (3.5) contributes more to your CGPA than a 2-credit course with an A.

## 6. Can I improve my SGPA?
Yes, you can enhance your Semester Grade Point Average by performing well in upcoming courses. Your SGPA is recalculated each semester based on current performance. Focus on subjects where you previously scored lower, and consider retaking courses to strengthen your overall average.

## 7. What are the consequences of missing course registration?
Missing registration means you'll lose access to required classes for that semester. You may need to wait until the next registration period or enroll in a reduced schedule. This could delay your graduation timeline and affect your ability to fulfill prerequisites for advanced courses.

## 8. Are internship credits mandatory for graduation?
Internship credits are not mandatory but strongly recommended. Students must complete at least 60 hours of work experience to earn 2 credit points. These credits count toward your total requirement but don't replace core academic courses. Internships provide valuable real-world skills that enhance employability.

## 9. How does revaluation work for exams?
Revaluation allows students to request a review of their exam papers if they believe there was an error in grading. Applications must be submitted within two weeks of results, along with supporting evidence. The process is handled by an independent committee, and decisions are final. Revaluation doesn't guarantee improved grades but ensures transparency in evaluation.

## 10. Can I graduate with backlogs?
Graduation requires the completion of all required courses without outstanding credits. Backlogged subjects must be cleared before you can receive your degree. If you have minor backlogs, you may be allowed to graduate provisionally, but this is subject to academic review and approval.

## 11. What is academic probation?
Academic probation is a status assigned to students who fail to meet minimum GPA requirements. Students on probation must achieve a higher GPA in the next semester to continue their program. This period allows for academic support and counseling to help students improve their performance.

## 12. How does semester progression work?
Semester progression requires students to complete a minimum number of credits each term to advance to the next level. Failure to meet these requirements may result in being held back or forced to repeat a semester. Students should consult their academic advisor to plan their course load effectively.

## 13. What are supplementary examinations?
Supplementary examinations are offered to students who fail a course but have completed all required coursework. These exams provide an opportunity to demonstrate understanding of the material. Eligibility is determined by the academic office, and there may be additional fees associated with these exams.

## 14. How do I add or drop courses after registration?
Students can add or drop courses within the first two weeks of the semester by submitting a formal request to the registrar's office. There are specific deadlines for each action, and approval is subject to availability of seats and academic policies. Dropping a course may affect your credit load and graduation timeline.

## 15. Can I change my elective after registration?
Elective changes are generally allowed only within the first week of classes. Students should consult their academic advisor to discuss options and ensure that any changes align with their degree requirements. Late changes may require special permission from the academic office.

## 16. What are prerequisites for courses?
Prerequisites are courses or skills required before enrolling in a more advanced course. They ensure students have the necessary foundation to succeed in higher-level coursework. Prerequisites are listed in the course syllabus and must be completed before registration.

## 17. How does credit transfer work from other institutions?
Credit transfer involves evaluating courses taken at another institution to determine if they can be applied toward your degree. The academic office reviews transcripts and course descriptions to assess equivalency. Transferred credits may affect your GPA calculation and graduation requirements.

## 18. What is a major project requirement?
Major projects are required for certain degrees and involve in-depth research or application of knowledge in a specific area. These projects are typically worth significant credit points and must be completed under faculty supervision. Students should plan early to meet deadlines and submit their work on time.

## 19. How do I request revaluation of my exam?
To request revaluation, students must submit an official form along with the exam paper to the academic office within two weeks of results. The process is handled by an independent committee, and decisions are final. Revaluation does not guarantee improved grades but ensures transparency in evaluation.

## 20. What are grace marks and how do they work?
Grace marks are additional points awarded to students for exceptional performance or special circumstances. They are typically applied to individual exams and may affect the final grade. Eligibility for grace marks is determined by academic policies and is subject to review by the relevant department.

## 21. What are graduation requirements for my degree?
Graduation requirements vary by program but generally include completing all required courses, maintaining a minimum GPA, and fulfilling any specific program mandates. Students should consult their academic advisor to ensure they meet all criteria before applying for graduation.

## 22. How do I apply for degree completion?

## 30. What are the consequences of failing multiple subjects?
Failing multiple subjects may result in academic probation or suspension from the program. Students on probation must achieve a higher GPA in the next semester to continue their studies. Repeated failures could lead to expulsion, so it's crucial to seek academic advising early and take steps to improve performance.

## 31. How do I appeal a failed course grade?
Students can appeal a failed course grade by submitting a formal request to the academic office within one month of results. The appeal must include evidence supporting the claim, such as medical records or proof of extenuating circumstances. A review panel will assess the case and decide whether to reconsider the grade.

## 32. What is the policy on late submissions for assignments?
Late submissions are generally not accepted without prior approval. Students who submit work after the deadline may receive a penalty, such as a reduction in marks or a failing grade. Exceptions can be made for documented emergencies, but these require submission of supporting documentation and approval from the instructor.

## 33. How do I request an extension for an assignment?
Students can request an extension for an assignment by contacting their instructor at least two days before the original deadline. The request should include a valid reason, such as illness or personal circumstances. Extensions are granted at the instructor's discretion and may come with conditions like submitting work in stages.

## 34. What is the policy on academic dishonesty?
Academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, cheating, and fabrication of data, is strictly prohibited. Students found guilty of these actions face disciplinary measures ranging from warnings to expulsion. All cases are reviewed by an academic integrity committee, and penalties depend on the severity of the offense.

## 35. How do I access my academic records?
Students can access their academic records through the university's online portal. This includes transcripts, course schedules, grades, and registration details. For physical copies or specific information not available digitally, students should contact the registrar's office for assistance.

## 36. What is the process for requesting a leave of absence?
To request a leave of absence, students must submit a formal application to the academic office at least one month before the intended start date. The application should include reasons for the leave and a plan for returning to studies. Approval requires review by the academic committee and may be granted for medical, personal, or other valid reasons.

## 37. How do I apply for financial aid or scholarships?
Students can apply for financial aid or scholarships by submitting an application through the university's financial aid office. Eligibility criteria vary by program, and applicants must meet specific requirements such as academic performance, financial need, or community service involvement. Applications are reviewed and awards are announced based on merit and availability.

## 38. What is the policy on using assistive technologies for students with disabilities?
Students with disabilities can request accommodations through the university's disability services office. These may include extended exam time, alternative formats for course materials, or specialized software tools. Students must provide documentation of their disability and submit an accommodation plan for approval.

## 39. How do I access campus resources like libraries and labs?
Campus resources such as libraries and laboratories are accessible to all enrolled students. Students can use these facilities by presenting a valid student ID. Some services may require prior registration or scheduling, especially for specialized equipment or study spaces.

## 40. What is the procedure for changing my major or minor?
Students wishing to change their major or minor must submit an application to the academic office. The process involves reviewing course requirements, ensuring compatibility with existing credits, and meeting any prerequisites for the new program. Approval is granted based on academic standing and availability of resources.

## 41. How do I request a transcript for external purposes?
Students can request an official transcript by submitting a form to the registrar's office. Transcripts are issued for educational purposes such as applying to graduate programs or employment opportunities. There may be a fee for expedited processing or international delivery, and students must provide valid identification when requesting transcripts.

## 42. What is the policy on academic integrity in research projects?
Academic integrity in research projects requires proper citation of sources, honest data collection, and ethical conduct throughout the research process. Students involved in collaborative projects must clearly define roles and contributions to avoid plagiarism or misrepresentation of work. Violations can result in disciplinary action.

## 43. How do I get support for mental health issues?
Students facing mental health challenges can access counseling services through the university's student support center. These services include individual counseling, group therapy, and crisis intervention. Students are encouraged to seek help early and maintain open communication with academic advisors for ongoing support.

## 44. What is the process for requesting a refund for dropped courses?
Students who drop courses after the add/drop period may be eligible for a partial refund, depending on the timing of the withdrawal. Refund policies vary by semester and are outlined in the university's financial guidelines. Students should contact the registrar's office to determine their eligibility and submit the necessary forms.

## 45. How do I access career counseling services?
Career counseling services are available through the university's career center. These include resume building, interview preparation, job search assistance, and internship opportunities. Students can schedule appointments with counselors to explore career paths and develop professional skills.

## 46. What is the policy on academic freedom for faculty?
Academic freedom allows faculty members to pursue research, publish findings, and teach without undue interference from external sources. This includes the right to choose teaching methods, select course content, and engage in scholarly activities. Faculty are expected to maintain ethical standards and uphold institutional values while exercising this freedom.

## 47. How do I report incidents of academic misconduct?
Students who witness or experience academic misconduct can report incidents through the university's integrity office. Reports should include details about the incident, names of individuals involved, and any supporting evidence. The integrity office investigates all allegations and takes appropriate disciplinary action based on findings.

## 48. What is the procedure for requesting a waiver of course requirements?

Waivers are granted for valid reasons such as prior learning, work experience, or exceptional circumstances. The request must include documentation and be reviewed by the relevant department for approval.

## 49. How do I access online learning resources?
Online learning resources are available through the university's virtual learning platform. These include course materials, discussion forums, video lectures, and interactive tools. Students should log in regularly to stay updated on assignments, participate in discussions, and access support from instructors and peers.

## 50. What is the policy on using mobile devices during classes?
The use of mobile devices during classes is generally restricted unless explicitly permitted by the instructor.
