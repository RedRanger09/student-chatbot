# University Grading System Guide

## Overview
The university grading system is a structured framework designed to evaluate student performance across academic semesters. This system ensures consistency, transparency, and fairness in assessing students' understanding of course material. It integrates internal assessments (assignments, quizzes, projects) with end-semester exams to provide a holistic view of student achievement. The grading scale converts raw scores into letter grades, which are then converted to grade points for computing academic performance indices like SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) and CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average).

## Internal Assessment
Internal assessments constitute 40% of the final evaluation and include:
- Weekly assignments (15%)
- Mid-term quizzes (10%)
- Class participation and attendance (10%)
- Minor projects/term papers (15%)

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Assignments:** Demonstrate conceptual understanding through problem-solving
- **Quizzes:** Assess knowledge retention of key topics
- **Participation:** Encourage active engagement in class discussions
- **Projects:** Evaluate application of theoretical concepts to real-world scenarios

Internal assessments are graded on a scale from 0 to 100, with the final score contributing directly to the student's SGPA. Instructors use rubrics to ensure objective evaluation of these components.

## End Semester Exams
End semester exams account for 60% of the final grade and typically include:
- Theory papers (45%)
- Practical/viva voce (15%)

**Exam Patterns:**
- Duration: 3 hours for theory papers, 2 hours for practical sessions
- Question types: Short answers, problem-solving, case studies
- Allowed aids: Non-programmable calculators, formula sheets

Exams are conducted in a proctored environment to maintain academic integrity. Results are calculated using an internal grading scale that maps raw scores to letter grades.

## Letter Grades
The grading system uses the following letter grade classifications:

| Grade | Description       | Minimum Score |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| A+    | Outstanding        | 90-100        |
| A     | Excellent          | 85-89         |
| A-    | Very Good          | 80-84         |
| B+    | Good               | 75-79         |
| B     | Satisfactory       | 70-74         |
| B-    | Fair               | 65-69         |
| C+    | Pass               | 60-64         |
| C     | Marginal Pass      | 55-59         |
| F     | Fail               | Below 55      |

Letter grades are assigned based on the weighted average of internal and external assessments.

## Grade Points
Each letter grade corresponds to a numerical value used for calculating SGPA/CGPA:

| Grade | Grade Point |
|-------|-------------|
| A+    | 10          |
| A     | 9           |
| A-    | 8           |
| B+    | 7           |
| B     | 6           |
| B-    | 5           |
| C+    | 4           |
| C     | 3           |
| F     | 0           |

Grade points are multiplied by credit hours for each course to calculate the semester GPA.

## SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average)
SGPA is calculated using the formula:
```
SGPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credit Hours) / Total Credit Hours
```
For example, a student with 3 courses (4 credits at B+, 3 credits at A, 2 credits at C+) would have:
```
SGPA = ((7×4)+(9×3)+(4×2))/9 = (28+27+8)/9 = 63/9 ≈ 7.0
```

## CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average)
CGPA is the average of all semester GPAs:
```
CGPA = Σ(SGPA × Credit Hours) / Total Credit Hours Across Semesters
```
This provides a long-term view of academic performance across multiple semesters.

## Grade Improvement Process
Students who believe their grades are incorrect can request revaluation through:
1. Submitting a formal application within 15 days of results
2. Paying the prescribed fee
3. Undergoing a re-evaluation by an independent committee
4. Receiving revised results within 30 days

Revaluation does not guarantee grade improvement and is subject to the reviewing committee's findings.

## Failed Courses
Courses with grades below C+ (i.e., SGPA < 3) are considered failed. Students must:
- Retake the course in subsequent semesters
- Earn a minimum of C+ in the retake
- Maintain an overall CGPA of 2.0 to remain in good academic standing

Failed courses impact both SGPA and CGPA calculations.

## Transcript
The academic transcript documents all coursework, grades, and credits earned. It includes:
- Course code and title
- Credit hours
- Grade received
- Grade point
- Instructors' names
- Semester details

Transcripts are issued annually and serve as official records of academic achievement.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Calculating SGPA
**Courses:**  
MATH101 (4 credits, A)  
PHYS102 (3 credits, B+)  
CHEM103 (2 credits, C+)  

```
SGPA = ((9×4)+(7×3)+(4×2))/9 = (36+21+8)/9 = 65/9 ≈ 7.2
```

### Example 2: CGPA Calculation
**Semester 1:** SGPA 7.0 (12 credits)  
**Semester 2:** SGPA 6.5 (14 credits)  

```
CGPA = ((7.0×12)+(6.5×14))/(12+14) = (84 + 91)/26 ≈ 175/26 ≈ 6.73
```

## FAQ

**Q1:** What is the passing criteria for a course?  
A: Students must earn at least a C+ (Grade Point ≥ 3) to pass a course.

**Q2:** Can I improve my grade after results are published?  
A: Yes, through the revaluation process described in the Grade Improvement section.

**Q3:** How is SGPA calculated for courses with practical components?  
A: Practical components are weighted according to their credit hours and contribute proportionally to the final SGPA.

**Q4:** What happens if I fail two consecutive semesters?  
A: Students failing two consecutive semesters may be placed on academic probation and required to attend a counseling session.

**Q5:** Can I retake a course without repeating it?  
A: No, students must retake the course in subsequent semesters to improve their grades.

**Q6:** How are failed courses treated in CGPA calculations?  
A: Failed courses (with Grade Point < 3) are excluded from CGPA calculations until successfully retaken.

**Q7:** What is the minimum CGPA required to graduate?  
A: A minimum CGPA of 2.0 is required for graduation, with no failed courses in the last two semesters.

**Q8:** Can I request revaluation for all my courses?  
A: Yes, but each revaluation request incurs a separate fee.

**Q9:** How long does the revaluation process take?  
A: The revaluation process typically takes 30 days from the date of submission.

**Q10:** What is the difference between SGPA and CGPA?  
A: SGPA reflects performance in a single semester, while CGPA represents overall academic performance across multiple semesters.

**Q11:** Can I get credit for prior learning?  
A: Yes, through recognized prior learning (RPL) assessments conducted by the university.

**Q12:** What is the grading scale for honors courses?  
A: Honors courses use a modified scale with additional grade distinctions (e.g., A++ for exceptional performance).

**Q13:** How are late submissions handled in internal assessments?  
A: Late submissions incur a 10% penalty unless prior approval is obtained from the instructor.

**Q14:** Can I appeal against my grades?  
A: Yes, through an official appeal process that must be initiated within 15 days of results publication.

**Q15:** What is the minimum attendance required for internal assessments?  
A: Students must attend at least 75% of classes to be eligible for internal assessment credits.

**Q16:** How are practical exams conducted?  
A: Practical exams are conducted in laboratory settings with strict adherence to safety protocols and evaluation criteria.

**Q17:** Can I use calculators during exams?  
A: Yes, non-programmable calculators are permitted in theory papers, but not in practical exams.

**Q18:** What is the grading policy for makeup exams?  
A: Makeup exams are conducted only for valid reasons (medical certificates, family emergencies) and carry a 20% penalty.

**Q19:** How are failed courses handled in the transcript?  
A: Failed courses are marked with an "F" until successfully retaken, at which point they are updated to reflect the new grade.

**Q20:** Can I change my major based on CGPA?  
A: Yes, students may switch majors based on academic performance, subject to departmental approval.

**Q21:** What is the grading policy for thesis/dissertation?  
A: Thesis work is graded on a scale from 0-100 with corresponding letter grades and grade points.

**Q22:** How are credit hours calculated for electives?  
A: Elective courses carry credit hours equivalent to their academic weight, typically ranging from 3 to 4 credits.

**Q23:** Can I get exemptions from certain courses?  
A: Exemptions may be granted for prior learning or professional experience, subject to departmental review.

**Q24:** What is the grading policy for summer/winter sessions?  
A: Summer/winter session grades are treated as regular semester grades and contribute to SGPA/CGPA calculations.

**Q25:** How can I improve my CGPA?  
A: Focus on high-grade courses, maintain consistent performance across semesters, and retake failed courses promptly.
