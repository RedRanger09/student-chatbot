# Admission FAQ

## Eligibility
- **Q: What are the eligibility criteria for admission?**  
  A: Applicants must hold a high school diploma or equivalent, demonstrate academic readiness through standardized test scores (SAT/ACT), and meet minimum GPA requirements (typically 3.0 on a 4.0 scale). International students must also prove English proficiency via TOEFL/IELTS.

- **Q: Can I apply if my high school isn't accredited?**  
  A: Yes, but you must provide additional documentation to verify coursework equivalency through official transcript evaluation services.

- **Q: Are there minimum subject requirements for applicants?**  
  A: Yes, you must complete at least three years of English, two years each of math and science, and one year each of history/social studies and a foreign language.

- **Q: Do I need to submit SAT/ACT scores if I'm applying to a test-optional program?**  
  A: No, but submitting scores may still strengthen your application for competitive programs.

- **Q: Can home-schooled students apply?**  
  A: Yes, provide official transcripts from your homeschooling institution and standardized test scores if applicable.

- **Q: What age requirements are there for applicants?**  
  A: Applicants must be at least 17 years old by the start of the academic year. Exceptions may be granted for mature students with significant life experience.

- **Q: Can part-time students apply?**  
  A: Yes, but you must demonstrate sufficient credit hours to complete your degree within the standard timeframe.

- **Q: Are there specific criteria for graduate programs?**  
  A: Graduate applicants must hold a bachelor's degree from an accredited institution and submit transcripts, letters of recommendation, and a statement of purpose.

## Documents
- **Q: What documents are required for the application?**  
  A: Required items include official high school transcripts, standardized test scores, recommendation letters (2–3), personal essay, and proof of English proficiency (for international applicants).

- **Q: Can I submit unofficial transcripts initially?**  
  A: Yes, but you must submit official transcripts by the final deadline to avoid application withdrawal.

- **Q: Do I need to translate my documents if they're in a language other than English?**  
  A: Yes, official translations from accredited agencies are required for non-English documents.

- **Q: Can I use third-party services to verify my academic credentials?**  
  A: Yes, services like WES (World Education Services) can authenticate international transcripts.

- **Q: What if my documents are not in English?**  
  A: You must submit certified translations along with the original documents.

- **Q: How should I format my personal essay?**  
  A: The essay should be 500–700 words, written in clear and concise language, and demonstrate your academic interests and career goals.

- **Q: What if I don't have official transcripts yet?**  
  A: Submit a provisional application with estimated grades, but ensure you provide official transcripts before the final deadline.

## Deadlines
- **Q: What are the key application deadlines?**  
  A: Early Action: November 1; Regular Decision: January 15; Final Deadline: March 1 (no late fees apply for regular decision).

- **Q: Can I submit my application after the final deadline?**  
  A: No, applications submitted after the final deadline will be considered incomplete and may be rejected.

- **Q: Are there extensions for international applicants?**  
  A: Yes, international students may request a 15-day extension to account for visa processing delays. Approval is not guaranteed.

- **Q: What if I need more time to complete my application?**  
  A: Contact the admissions office to request an extension before the final deadline. Documented extenuating circumstances may qualify for consideration.

## Payments
- **Q: How do I pay the application fee?**  
  A: The non-refundable application fee is $75. You can pay online via credit card or PayPal through our admissions portal.

- **Q: Can I request a fee waiver?**  
  A: Yes, submit a financial aid form to request a waiver for low-income applicants.

- **Q: What happens if I miss the payment deadline?**  
  A: Late payments will incur a $25 fee and may result in application withdrawal.

- **Q: Are there payment plan options available?**  
  A: Yes, contact the financial aid office to discuss installment payment plans for tuition and fees.

## Scholarships
- **Q: Are there scholarships available for incoming students?**  
  A: Yes, merit-based and need-based scholarships are awarded. Apply through the financial aid section of your application.

- **Q: How do I apply for institutional scholarships?**  
  A: Complete the FAFSA and CSS Profile to be considered for need-based awards. Merit scholarships require a separate application with academic achievements.

- **Q: Can I receive both merit and need-based scholarships?**  
  A: Yes, but scholarship amounts may be adjusted based on your financial situation and available funding.

- **Q: Are scholarships renewable?**  
  A: Most scholarships are renewable for up to four years, provided you maintain satisfactory academic progress and meet program requirements.

## International Applicants
- **Q: What additional requirements do international students have?**  
  A: Proof of English proficiency (TOEFL/IELTS), visa documentation, and an interview may be required. Check the international student portal for specifics.

- **Q: Can I work while studying in the U.S.?**  
  A: Yes, but you must maintain full-time enrollment and obtain Optional Practical Training (OPT) authorization.

- **Q: How long does it take to process my visa application?**  
  A: Visa processing times vary by country. Apply at least 3–4 months before your intended start date to allow for delays.

- **Q: Do I need a tuberculosis test?**  
  A: Yes, most international students must provide proof of a negative TB test result as part of the visa application process.

## Transfers
- **Q: Can I transfer credits from another institution?**  
  A: Yes, submit an official transcript for evaluation. Up to 30 credits may be transferred toward your degree.

- **Q: What is the deadline for transferring credits?**  
  A: Transfer credits must be submitted by the end of the first semester to ensure eligibility for financial aid and scholarships.

- **Q: How are transfer credits evaluated?**  
  A: Credits are evaluated based on equivalency with our curriculum. Courses taken within the last five years are typically accepted.

- **Q: Can I apply for credit for prior learning (CPL)?**  
  A: Yes, submit a portfolio or documentation of work experience to be assessed for credit through the Office of Academic Affairs.

## Deferrals
- **Q: Can I defer my admission to a future term?**  
  A: Yes, submit a deferral request form by April 1. You'll need to reapply for the next academic year.

- **Q: What if I'm unable to attend due to unforeseen circumstances?**  
  A: Contact the admissions office immediately to discuss alternatives, including deferment or withdrawal policies.

- **Q: Can I defer my admission more than once?**  
  A: Deferrals are granted on a case-by-case basis. Multiple deferrals may affect your eligibility for financial aid and scholarships.

## Application Edits
- **Q: Can I edit my application after submission?**  
  A: Minor corrections are allowed within one week of submission. Major changes require approval from the admissions office.

- **Q: What constitutes a major change to my application?**  
  A: Significant changes to academic records, personal essays, or recommendation letters may require re-evaluation of your application.

- **Q: Can I add new information after submitting my application?**  
  A: Yes, but you must request approval from the admissions office before making any additions or modifications.

## Status Tracking
- **Q: How can I check the status of my application?**  
  A: Log in to your applicant portal for real-time updates on review progress and decision timelines.

- **Q: What if I don't receive a response within the expected timeframe?**  
  A: Contact the admissions office via email or phone to inquire about the status of your application.

- **Q: Can I request an expedited review of my application?**  
  A: Yes, but this is only available for applicants with documented extenuating circumstances. Approval is not guaranteed.

## Appeals
- **Q: What if I'm denied admission?**  
  A: You may appeal the decision by submitting a written request with supporting documentation within 30 days of receiving the decision.

- **Q: What are the grounds for an appeal?**  
  A: Appeals may be granted for errors in evaluation, incomplete information, or extenuating circumstances affecting your application.

- **Q: How long does the appeals process take?**  
  A: The appeals review typically takes 2–4 weeks. You will receive a written response detailing the outcome.

## Common Mistakes
- **Q: What are common mistakes applicants make?**  
  A: Incomplete applications, incorrect document formatting, and late submissions. Always review your application for errors before final submission.

- **Q: What if I submit documents in the wrong format?**  
  A: Documents must be submitted as PDFs or scanned images. Incorrect formats may result in delays or rejection of your application.

- **Q: Can I use abbreviations in my application materials?**  
  A: Avoid excessive use of abbreviations unless they are widely recognized. Always spell out acronyms on first mention.

- **Q: What if I'm unsure about a question on the application?**  
  A: Contact the admissions office for clarification before submitting your application to avoid misunderstandings.
