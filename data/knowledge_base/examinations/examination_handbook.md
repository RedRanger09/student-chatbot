# Examination Handbook

## Overview
This handbook outlines the policies, procedures, and guidelines governing all examinations at Greenfield University. It provides essential information for students to understand their responsibilities, prepare effectively, and navigate the examination process with clarity. The document includes details on eligibility criteria, exam conduct rules, submission procedures, and support mechanisms for special circumstances.

## Examination System
The university employs a structured examination system designed to assess student learning outcomes comprehensively. Examinations are categorized into **semester examinations**, **mid-semester examinations**, **practical examinations**, and **end-semester examinations**. Each category serves distinct academic purposes:

- **Semester Examinations**: Cover major subjects taught during the semester, evaluating conceptual understanding and application.
- **Mid-Semester Examinations**: Assess progress midway through the term, providing feedback for improvement.
- **Practical Examinations**: Focus on hands-on skills in laboratory courses, clinical training, or fieldwork.
- **End-Semester Examinations**: Final assessments of cumulative knowledge across all topics covered in the course.

### Examination Schedule
The academic calendar includes a detailed examination schedule published two weeks before the start of each semester. Key dates include:
- **Admit Card Release**: 15 days prior to exams.
- **Exam Start Date**: As specified in the schedule.
- **Result Declaration**: 10 working days after exams.

Students must refer to their department’s portal for personalized schedules and updates.

## Internal Assessment
Internal assessment contributes up to 30% of the final grade. It includes:
- Regular quizzes (20%)
- Assignments and projects (10%)

All submissions must adhere to deadlines and formatting guidelines provided by instructors. Late submissions without valid justification will receive a maximum score of 50%.

## Semester Examinations
Semester examinations are conducted in February and July for most programs. These exams account for 70% of the final grade and cover all topics taught during the semester.

### Eligibility Criteria
Students must meet these requirements to appear:
- Minimum attendance: 75% (excluding excused absences)
- Submit all assignments on time
- Clear pending academic disciplinary actions

## Mid-Semester Examinations
Mid-semester exams are held in November for undergraduate programs and October for postgraduate students. These assessments focus on core concepts and help identify areas needing improvement.

### Key Policies
- **Exam Duration**: 2 hours for most subjects; longer for practical papers.
- **Allowed Materials**: Only the exam paper, writing instruments, and permitted calculators (as specified).
- **Prohibited Items**: Mobile phones, smartwatches, and unauthorized electronic devices are strictly prohibited. Possession may result in immediate disqualification.

## Practical Examinations
Practical exams evaluate hands-on skills and are conducted in labs, clinics, or field settings. Students must:
- Arrive 15 minutes early to set up equipment.
- Follow safety protocols (e.g., wearing protective gear).
- Complete all required tasks within the allocated time.

### Grading Criteria
Assessment includes:
- Technical accuracy (60%)
- Efficiency in task completion (30%)
- Adherence to safety standards (10%)

## End-Semester Examinations
End-semester exams are held in April and September. These exams carry the highest weightage (70%) and require comprehensive preparation.

### Special Accommodations
Students with disabilities or medical conditions can request accommodations through the Disability Support Office. Approved requests may include:
- Extended time (up to 50% additional)
- Use of assistive technology
- Alternative exam formats

## Admit Cards
Admit cards are issued 15 days before exams and must be printed in black-and-white. Key details include:
- Student name, roll number, and course code
- Exam date, time, and venue
- Instructions for entry (e.g., ID verification)

### Admit Card Guidelines
- **Printing**: Print on A4 paper using a laser printer.
- **Submission**: Submit the admit card to the exam center 3 days before the exam.
- **Lost Cards**: Replacement cards can be obtained at the examination office with a fee of ₹50.

## Examination Eligibility
Students are eligible to appear if:
- They have completed all prerequisite courses.
- Their attendance meets the minimum threshold (75%).
- They have no pending academic disciplinary actions.

### Exemption Policies
Students who miss more than 25% of classes without valid reasons will be ineligible for exams. Valid reasons include:
- Medical certification from a registered doctor.
- Family emergencies documented with official proof.
- Government service obligations.

## Attendance Requirements
Regular attendance is mandatory for all examinations. Students must attend at least 75% of classes to be eligible for exams. For online courses, participation in virtual classes and discussion forums counts toward attendance.

### Attendance Verification
Attendance is tracked through:
- Physical sign-in (for on-campus students)
- Online platforms (for remote learners)
- In-class quizzes and assignments

## Required Documents
On the day of the exam, students must carry:
- Valid ID proof (passport, Aadhaar card, or university ID).
- Admit card.
- Stationery (pencils, erasers, a pen for writing).
- Calculator (only if permitted).

### Identity Verification
All candidates must undergo identity verification at the start of exams. This includes:
- Matching the admit card details with physical documents.
- Fingerprint scanning in high-security exam centers.

## Examination Instructions
**Reporting Time**: Arrive 30 minutes before the exam starts to complete registration and locate your seat.

**Exam Hall Rules**:
- No entry after the first bell.
- Maintain silence during exams.
- Do not share materials with other candidates.
- Raise your hand for any clarification.

### Late Arrival Policy
Students arriving more than 15 minutes late will not be allowed to sit for the exam. Exceptions apply for:
- Medical emergencies (with valid documentation).
- Transportation delays documented by the university transport office.

## Writing Guidelines
- Use blue or black ink for all answers.
- Write legibly; unclear handwriting may result in partial marks.
- Follow specific formatting rules for essays, problem-solving questions, and multiple-choice formats.

### Answer Booklet Rules
- Fill in your details (name, roll number) on the cover page.
- Do not use correction fluid or tape. Use a single line to correct mistakes.
- Separate answer sheets must be attached for each section.

## Submission Procedure
After completing exams:
- Hand over the answer booklet to invigilators.
- Do not carry any papers out of the exam hall.
- Report any discrepancies immediately to the proctor.

### Missed Examinations
Students who miss an exam without valid reasons will receive a score of zero. Valid reasons include:
- Medical emergencies (with hospital certification).
- Family emergencies with police reports or official letters.
- Participation in authorized competitions (e.g., national science fairs).

## Medical Emergencies
In case of sudden illness during exams, students must:
1. Immediately inform the invigilator.
2. Provide a medical certificate within 7 days.
3. Submit an application for re-evaluation through the academic office.

### Emergency Contacts
- University Health Center: +91-1234567890
- Student Counseling Services: +91-1234567891

## Special Accommodations
Students with disabilities or chronic illnesses can request accommodations by:
1. Submitting a formal application to the Disability Support Office.
2. Providing medical documentation from a licensed practitioner.
3. Attending an assessment session to discuss specific needs.

### Approved Accommodations
Examples include:
- Extended exam time (up to 50% additional).
- Use of screen readers or Braille materials.
- Quiet examination rooms for neurodiverse students.

## Examination Results
Results are declared within 10 working days after exams. Students can access them via the university portal using their login credentials. Key details include:
- Subject-wise scores.
- Grade distribution (e.g., A, B+, C).
- Remarks on performance.

### Supplementary Examinations
Students who fail a subject may appear for supplementary exams in the next academic session. These exams are conducted in August and February. To qualify:
- Achieve at least 35% in the main exam.
- Submit an application within one week of results.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Not reading instructions**: Missing key guidelines like ID verification or writing rules can lead to disqualification.
2. **Arriving late**: Missing the reporting time may result in automatic failure.
3. **Using unauthorized materials**: Possession of mobile phones or calculators (if not permitted) is grounds for penalty.

## Student Responsibilities
- Prepare thoroughly by attending classes and reviewing notes.
- Adhere to examination rules and maintain academic integrity.
- Report any irregularities immediately to authorities.
- Respect examiners and fellow students during the process.
