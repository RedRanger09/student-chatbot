# Examination Malpractice Policy

## Overview  
This policy outlines the definitions, procedures, and consequences related to academic malpractice during examinations at [University Name]. Academic integrity is a cornerstone of scholarly pursuit, and all students are expected to uphold the highest standards of honesty and fairness. This document clarifies what constitutes examination misconduct, the investigative process, disciplinary actions, and preventive measures to ensure a equitable assessment environment.

---

## Types of Malpractice  

### 1. Cheating  
Cheating involves using unauthorized means to gain an unfair advantage, including:  
- **Copying** answers from other students' papers or digital devices  
- **Impersonation** by having another student take the exam in your place  
- **Mobile phone usage** for accessing notes, communication, or external resources  
- **Smart device tampering** (e.g., hidden calculators, Bluetooth-enabled gadgets)  
- **Unauthorized notes** in pockets, bags, or on desks  

### 2. Communication During Exams  
Any form of verbal or written exchange with others during an exam constitutes malpractice, including:  
- Passing notes  
- Whispering answers  
- Using electronic devices to send/receive messages  

### 3. Plagiarism in Practical Exams  
Submitting work that is not original, including:  
- Copying lab reports or code from external sources  
- Fabricating data or results  

### 4. Digital Malpractice  
Violations related to technology use during exams include:  
- Using browser extensions or scripts to automate tasks  
- Accessing unauthorized websites or apps  
- Circumventing proctoring software  

---

## Investigation Process  
1. **Reporting**: Any faculty member, student, or staff may report suspected malpractice.  
2. **Evidence Collection**: Digital footprints, surveillance footage, and witness statements are reviewed.  
3. **Committee Review**: A disciplinary committee (comprising faculty and student representatives) evaluates the evidence.  
4. **Student Rights**: Students have the right to:  
   - Request a summary of allegations  
   - Provide explanations or counter-evidence  
   - Appeal the decision  

---

## Penalties  
Disciplinary actions are proportionate to the severity of the offense:  

### 1. Warnings  
- First-time minor offenses (e.g., accidental use of a mobile phone) may result in a formal warning.  

### 2. Exam Cancellation  
- Students found cheating may have their exam invalidated, requiring retake under supervised conditions.  

### 3. Suspension  
- Repeat offenders or those involved in organized cheating face temporary suspension from academic activities.  

### 4. Expulsion  
- Severe cases (e.g., impersonation, large-scale plagiarism) may lead to permanent expulsion.  

---

## Appeals  
Students dissatisfied with disciplinary decisions may appeal within **10 business days** by submitting a written request to the Academic Integrity Office. The appeal must include:  
- A clear explanation of why the decision is unjust  
- Supporting evidence or witness statements  

---

## Prevention  
### Guidelines for Students  
- **Avoid distractions**: Store mobile devices in locked bags during exams.  
- **Use approved materials**: Only permitted resources (e.g., calculators, formula sheets) may be used.  
- **Clarify doubts**: Ask instructors before the exam begins if unsure about rules.  

### Common Misconceptions  
- **"Accidental cheating" is not an excuse** – even unintentional violations are treated seriously.  
- **"I didn’t know it was against the rules"** – responsibility lies with students to understand policies.  
- **"Sharing notes for study purposes"** – collaboration during exams is strictly prohibited.
