# Library Handbook

## Overview  
The University Central Library serves as a vital academic resource, offering students and faculty access to extensive collections, study spaces, and digital tools. Our mission is to foster learning, research, and innovation by providing an environment that supports intellectual growth and discovery.

### Library Objectives  
- Provide equitable access to information resources  
- Support academic excellence through curated collections  
- Promote literacy and lifelong learning  
- Encourage collaborative scholarship through technology-enabled services  

### Locations  
**Main Library**: Building A, Ground Floor | Open 24/7 during exam periods  
**Science Wing**: Building C, 3rd Floor | Specialized STEM resources  
**Law & Humanities**: Building D, 2nd Floor | Legal research materials  

### Working Hours  
- **Monday-Friday**: 8:00 AM - 10:00 PM  
- **Saturday**: 9:00 AM - 6:00 PM  
- **Sunday**: Closed (except emergencies)  

## Membership  
All enrolled students automatically receive a library card. Faculty and staff must apply in person.  

### Eligibility  
- Full-time undergraduate/graduate students  
- Teaching staff with university ID  
- Visiting scholars with valid affiliation  

### Card Features  
- Borrowing privileges (see *Borrowing Overview*)  
- OPAC access  
- Study room reservations  
- Interlibrary loan capabilities  

## Library Collections  
Our collections span traditional and digital formats, supporting diverse academic needs.  

### Book Collections  
Over 250,000 volumes across disciplines, with special emphasis on:  
- Humanities & Social Sciences  
- STEM fields (including lab manuals)  
- Business & Economics  
- Law & Politics  

### Reference Books  
Curated collections in the reference section include:  
- Encyclopedias and dictionaries  
- Statistical yearbooks  
- Subject-specific handbooks  
- Government publications  

### Journals & Periodicals  
Access to over 5,000 academic journals via subscription. Highlights include:  
- Peer-reviewed research publications  
- Industry reports  
- Legal case law databases  
- Current affairs magazines  

### Digital Collections  
- E-books and audiobooks through the University's digital repository  
- Subscription-based databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, etc.)  
- Archival digitization projects  
- Open-access academic resources  

## Services  
We offer a range of services to enhance your research experience.  

### OPAC (Online Catalog)  
Search our collections using [OPAC](https://opac.university.edu). Features include:  
- Advanced search filters by author, subject, and format  
- Availability tracking  
- Renewal options  
- Request for interlibrary loans  

### Study Spaces  
- **Reading Halls**: Silent study areas with seating for 300+ students  
- **Group Study Rooms**: Bookable via the reservation system (see *Reservation System*)  
- **Computer Labs**: 24/7 access to research stations with internet and citation tools  

### Borrowing Overview  
- Maximum of 5 items per student  
- Renewals allowed up to 3 times (see *Renewal Policies*)  
- Fines apply for late returns ($0.50/day per item)  

### Reservation System  
Secure your study space or meeting room through the online portal. Priority given to:  
- Final-year students  
- Research groups  
- Faculty members  

## Rules  
To maintain a productive environment, all users must adhere to these guidelines.  

### Library Etiquette  
- Maintain quiet in reading halls (no phone calls)  
- Use headphones in group study areas  
- No food or drinks in designated reading spaces  
- Respect copyright laws when using digital resources  

### Computer Access  
- Personal devices must be charged before use  
- Printing privileges limited to 50 pages/day  
- Software installation requires prior approval  

### Emergency Procedures  
- Fire alarms: Evacuate immediately via nearest stairwell  
- Medical emergencies: Call campus security (extension 412)  
- Power outages: Use emergency lighting in main lobby  

## Student Responsibilities  
You are responsible for:  
- Returning items by due date  
- Reporting damaged equipment  
- Respecting intellectual property rights  
- Maintaining your library card privileges  

## Common Mistakes  
- **Not renewing books on time**: Leads to fines and restricted access  
- **Using study rooms without reservation**: May result in loss of booking privileges  
- **Ignoring citation guidelines**: Increases risk of plagiarism accusations  
- **Overloading OPAC searches**: Use specific keywords for better results  

## Important Reminders  
1. Lost or damaged books must be reported within 24 hours  
2. Digital resources require valid login credentials  
3. Study room bookings expire 10 minutes after reservation time  
4. Interlibrary loan requests take 5-7 business days to process
