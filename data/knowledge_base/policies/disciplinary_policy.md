# Disciplinary Policy

## Overview
The [University Name] Student Disciplinary Policy establishes clear expectations for responsible behavior within the academic community. This policy outlines procedures for addressing misconduct, ensuring fairness through documented investigations, and providing students with opportunities to appeal decisions. The university is committed to maintaining an environment that promotes integrity, respect, and academic excellence.

---

## Types of Misconduct

### Minor Misconduct
Behavior that disrupts the educational mission but does not significantly harm institutional interests:
- Disregarding classroom etiquette (e.g., repeated disruptions)
- Unauthorized use of campus facilities
- Minor violations of dress code policies
- Failure to complete assigned community service hours

**Example:** A student repeatedly uses their phone during lectures, disrupting learning for peers. This constitutes minor misconduct requiring a formal warning.

### Major Misconduct
Actions that seriously undermine institutional operations or safety:
- Threatening staff or fellow students
- Unauthorized access to restricted systems
- Fraudulent use of university resources
- Harassment or bullying behavior
- Violent conduct on campus property

**Example:** A student creates and distributes fake exam answers, undermining academic integrity. This major misconduct warrants disciplinary action.

### Academic Misconduct
Violations of scholarly standards:
- Plagiarism (submitting others' work as original)
- Fabrication of data or sources
- Examination fraud (cheating, impersonation)
- Unauthorized collaboration on assignments
- Misrepresentation of credentials

**Example:** A student submits a paper with 30% plagiarized content without proper citations. This academic misconduct may result in grade penalties and disciplinary action.

### Non-Academic Misconduct
Behavior affecting campus environment:
- Disruptive behavior at events
- Unauthorized parking violations
- Failure to comply with emergency protocols
- Violent or threatening language
- Discriminatory harassment

**Example:** A student uses racist slurs during a public event, creating a hostile environment. This non-academic misconduct requires immediate intervention.

## Investigation Process

### Complaint Initiation
Any faculty member, staff, or student may file a formal complaint through the Office of Student Conduct. Written documentation is required, including:
- Clear description of alleged misconduct
- Dates/times of incidents
- Supporting evidence (photos, witness statements)
- Relevant policy references

**Note:** Anonymous complaints will be reviewed if they contain sufficient detail to initiate an investigation.

### Investigation Procedures
The Director of Student Conduct leads investigations using:
1. **Evidence Collection**: Reviewing digital records, witness interviews, and physical evidence
2. **Interviews**: Conducting formal statements with all parties involved
3. **Documentation**: Maintaining a confidential case file throughout the process
4. **Expert Consultation**: Involving faculty experts for complex academic misconduct cases

**Example:** A plagiarism allegation would involve checking source citations, comparing documents, and interviewing the student about their research process.

### Student Rights During Investigation
- Right to review all evidence against them
- Right to present witnesses and documentation
- Right to request a hearing within 10 business days
- Right to legal counsel (at own expense)
- Protection from retaliation for cooperating with investigations

## Penalties & Sanctions

### Warnings
First-time minor infractions typically result in:
- Written warning documented in student record
- Mandatory attendance at conduct workshops
- Required completion of a reflective essay

**Example:** A first-time violation of dress code policies results in a formal warning and mandatory attire guidelines review.

### Probation
For more serious misconduct, students may be placed on probation with conditions:
- Academic probation: Must maintain minimum GPA requirements
- Behavioral probation: Must complete designated community service hours
- Curfew restrictions: Limited access to campus facilities after specified times

**Example:** A student found responsible for vandalism is placed on behavioral probation requiring 20 hours of volunteer work.

### Suspension
Temporary removal from campus for:
- 1-3 days for urgent safety concerns
- 7-14 days for moderate violations
- 1-3 academic terms for severe misconduct

**Example:** A student involved in a violent incident may face immediate suspension pending investigation.

### Expulsion
Permanent removal for:
- Repeated major infractions
- Criminal behavior on campus
- Threats to institutional safety
- Academic fraud with intent to deceive

**Example:** A student found guilty of selling exam answers multiple times would face expulsion.

---

## Appeals Process

Students may appeal disciplinary decisions within 15 business days by submitting:
1. Written notice of appeal
2. Supporting documentation
3. Payment of $50 processing fee
4. Optional legal representation

Appeals are reviewed by an independent committee that may:
- Confirm original decision
- Modify penalties
- Require additional evidence review
- Schedule a re-hearing

**Note:** Appeals do not guarantee reversal of decisions but provide opportunity for reconsideration.

---

## Student Responsibilities

1. **Compliance**: Adhere to all published policies and procedures
2. **Accountability**: Accept responsibility for actions through appropriate channels
3. **Documentation**: Maintain accurate records of disciplinary history
4. **Appeals**: Use formal processes for challenging decisions
5. **Confidentiality**: Respect privacy of others' disciplinary records

**Example:** Students must complete required conduct workshops within 30 days of notification.

---

## Record Keeping

Disciplinary records are maintained for:
- 7 years for minor infractions
- 10 years for major violations
- Indefinitely for expulsion records

Students may request access to their records through the Office of Student Conduct. Records are sealed upon completion of all sanctions.

---

## Confidentiality

All disciplinary proceedings are confidential:
- Records are accessible only to authorized personnel
- Information sharing requires written consent
- Public disclosure is prohibited without approval
- Exceptions apply for safety-related disclosures

**Example:** A student's misconduct record cannot be shared with employers without their explicit permission.
