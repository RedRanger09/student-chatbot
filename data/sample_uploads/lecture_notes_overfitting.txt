Lecture Notes: Supervised Learning and Model Generalization
============================================================

1. Introduction
---------------
Supervised learning trains a model on labeled examples so it can predict
labels for unseen inputs. The central goal is generalization: performing
well on data that was not part of the training set.

2. Overfitting
--------------
Overfitting happens when a model learns the training data too closely,
including noise and spurious patterns. An overfitted model shows low
training error but high test error. Typical causes include a model that
is too complex for the amount of data, training for too many epochs, or
insufficient regularization.

Signs of overfitting:
- Training accuracy keeps improving while validation accuracy stalls or drops
- Very large weights in linear models
- Decision trees that grow extremely deep

3. Underfitting
---------------
Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying
structure of the data. Both training and test error remain high.
Increasing model capacity or improving features can help.

4. Bias-Variance Tradeoff
-------------------------
Bias is error from overly simplistic assumptions. Variance is error from
sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set. Overfitting is
associated with high variance; underfitting with high bias. Regularization,
cross-validation, and more training data help manage this tradeoff.

5. Techniques to Reduce Overfitting
-----------------------------------
- Cross-validation to select hyperparameters
- Early stopping during neural network training
- Dropout, weight decay (L2), and data augmentation
- Pruning decision trees and limiting tree depth
- Collecting more representative training examples

6. Evaluation Metrics
---------------------
Always evaluate on a held-out test set. For classification, accuracy,
precision, recall, and F1-score are common. For regression, RMSE and MAE
are widely used. Never tune hyperparameters on the final test set.
